# SWOT Analysis

## What It Is
SWOT is a strategic analysis framework that evaluates an organization or initiative across four dimensions: Strengths (internal, positive), Weaknesses (internal, negative), Opportunities (external, positive), and Threats (external, negative). Extended with the TOWS matrix, it generates strategies by combining internal and external factors. Widely used since the 1960s for strategic planning.

## When to Use
- Strategic planning sessions to assess current position
- Evaluating new market entry, product launches, or partnerships
- Competitive analysis to identify advantages and vulnerabilities
- Team or organizational retrospectives at a strategic level

## When NOT to Use
- Feature prioritization — too strategic for sprint-level decisions
- When you need quantitative analysis — SWOT is qualitative
- As a standalone strategy tool — SWOT identifies factors but doesn't prescribe strategy without TOWS

## How to Apply
1. **Strengths**: What do we do well? Resources, capabilities, competitive advantages, brand, team, technology, IP
2. **Weaknesses**: Where do we fall short? Gaps, resource constraints, technical debt, skill gaps, reputation issues
3. **Opportunities**: What external factors can we exploit? Market trends, regulatory changes, competitor mistakes, new technologies, unmet needs
4. **Threats**: What external factors could hurt us? Competitors, market shifts, regulation, economic conditions, technology disruption
5. **TOWS Matrix** (strategy generation):
   - **SO** (Strengths + Opportunities): How can we use strengths to exploit opportunities?
   - **WO** (Weaknesses + Opportunities): How can we overcome weaknesses to exploit opportunities?
   - **ST** (Strengths + Threats): How can we use strengths to defend against threats?
   - **WT** (Weaknesses + Threats): How can we minimize weaknesses to avoid threats?

## Example
**Company**: Mid-stage SaaS analytics company
- **S**: Strong data visualization engine, loyal SMB customer base, fast iteration speed
- **W**: No enterprise features (SSO, RBAC), small sales team, limited brand recognition
- **O**: Enterprise analytics market growing 25%/year, competitors raising prices, AI hype driving analytics demand
- **T**: Large incumbents (Tableau, Looker) moving downmarket, economic downturn reducing budgets, AI tools potentially commoditizing dashboards
- **SO Strategy**: Use visualization strength + AI demand to launch AI-powered insights feature (differentiation)
- **WT Strategy**: Build enterprise features before incumbents capture our SMB customers moving upmarket

## Key Pitfalls
- Listing too many items — focus on the top 3–5 per quadrant
- Not being honest about weaknesses — the value is in candor
- Stopping at the SWOT grid without generating strategies (TOWS) — identification without action is useless
- Confusing internal and external — "competition" is external (threat), not a weakness

## Related Frameworks
- **PESTEL** — detailed external analysis that feeds into Opportunities and Threats
- **Porter's Five Forces** — competitive analysis feeding the Threats dimension
- **Value Chain Analysis** — internal analysis feeding Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Ansoff Matrix** — growth strategies that emerge from TOWS analysis
