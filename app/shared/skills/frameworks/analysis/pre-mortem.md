# Pre-Mortem

## What It Is
The Pre-Mortem, created by psychologist Gary Klein, is a risk identification technique that asks the team to imagine it's the future and the project has failed. From that vantage point, participants write down all the reasons it failed. This reversal overcomes the optimism bias that makes teams downplay risks during planning and surfaces concerns people might otherwise hesitate to voice.

## When to Use
- Before committing to a major initiative, launch, or strategy
- When the team seems overconfident about a plan's success
- As a complement to project kickoffs or sprint planning for high-risk work
- When stakeholders have concerns they haven't voiced

## When NOT to Use
- Very low-risk work where the exercise adds no value
- When the team is already pessimistic or demoralized — a pre-mortem will amplify negativity
- As a substitute for ongoing risk management — it's a point-in-time exercise

## How to Apply
1. **Set the stage**: "Imagine it's [6 months from now]. This project has failed spectacularly. Not just fell short — failed."
2. **Individual brainstorm** (5–10 minutes): Each person independently writes down all the reasons the project failed. Be specific
3. **Share and compile** (15 minutes): Go around the room. Each person shares one reason at a time (round-robin prevents groupthink). Record all reasons
4. **Cluster and prioritize**: Group related reasons. Vote on which failure modes are most likely and most damaging
5. **Mitigate**: For the top 3–5 risks, define specific prevention or contingency actions
6. **Integrate**: Add mitigations to your project plan. Assign owners

## Example
**Project**: Launch enterprise tier with self-serve contract signing
**Pre-Mortem Results (top 5)**:
1. Legal approved the contract template but it didn't cover data residency → Mitigation: Legal review of EU-specific clauses before launch
2. Self-serve billing failed for invoiced customers → Mitigation: Test invoice flow with 3 existing enterprise customers pre-launch
3. Enterprise customers expected dedicated onboarding that we didn't staff → Mitigation: Hire 1 enterprise CSM before launch; define onboarding SLA
4. Competitors undercut pricing during our launch month → Mitigation: Prepare competitive response messaging; focus on value, not price
5. SSO integration broke during peak signup period → Mitigation: Load test SSO flow; have on-call engineering during launch week

## Key Pitfalls
- Not creating psychological safety — people won't share real concerns if they fear judgment
- Doing it too late (after commitments are made) — do it early enough to change course
- Listing risks but not defining mitigations — identification without action is useless
- Only doing it once — revisit the pre-mortem at major milestones to check for new risks

## Related Frameworks
- **Inversion** — the general mental model that pre-mortems apply specifically to projects
- **Assumption Mapping** — related technique for identifying what must be true
- **SPADE** — pre-mortems can inform the risk assessment in the Alternatives step
- **Root Cause Analysis** — post-mortem version applied to actual failures
